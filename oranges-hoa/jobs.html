The jobs, made small

The association is 33 homes run by neighbors who volunteer. This page shows each job, what the website now does for it, and what is honestly left for a person. The aim is that nobody has to carry much.

The jobs

President

Today: Maureen Sanelli

Presides at board meetings and sees that the board’s decisions are carried out.

The website does
  • Posts any email you mark for the website
  • Keeps the next meeting, with its join link, on the home page
  • Answers the common questions neighbors used to email about: trash, dues address, rules
Left for a person
  • Run the monthly meeting
  • Decide what the board decides
  • Forward the emails worth sharing

Our estimate: the meeting, plus a few minutes a week

Vice President

Today: Christine Lucas

Acts for the President when needed and supports board projects.

The website does
  • Gives a stand-in the same picture the President has: calendar, notices, open requests in one place
Left for a person
  • Step in when the President is away
  • Take one project a year, such as a common-area repair

Our estimate: the meeting, plus the project

Secretary

Today: Lyuba Tartova Maybury

Minutes, records, meeting notices, and the website.

The website does
  • Builds the monthly newsletter by itself
  • Puts meeting dates on the calendar from an email subject line
  • Shows the “last updated” date so nobody has to check for stale pages
  • Has the meeting notice already written (below)
Left for a person
  • Take minutes and drop the file in the Documents folder
  • Send the annual meeting notice at least 15 days ahead

Our estimate: the meeting, plus about half an hour a month

Treasurer

Today: Morgan Hoover

Dues, budget, financial statements, and the annual audit.

The website does
  • Tells every neighbor where to send the check and what to write on it
  • Explains the late fee in plain words, so fewer people ask
  • Has the dues reminder already written (below)
  • When accounts are switched on: each home sees its own paid or unpaid status
Left for a person
  • Send invoices, deposit checks, pay bills
  • Keep the books and hand them to the auditor
  • Present the budget at the annual meeting

Our estimate: steady through the year, heaviest at invoice time and before the annual meeting

Exterior requests

Architectural Control Committee

Reviews requests for fences, paint, roofs, decks and sheds.

The website does
  • Takes each request through one form, so they all arrive the same way
  • Shows neighbors where their request stands, by initials only
  • Has the “we received it” reply already written (below)
Left for a person
  • Look at the request and decide
  • Pick the new status from a list

Our estimate: about fifteen minutes per request

Ways to share the work

One inbox, three labels

Everything arrives at the board’s one email address. Whoever opens it drags each email onto a label: Website, Treasurer, or Requests. The right person finds their pile without anyone forwarding or asking.

Small jobs that do not need a board seat

Some neighbors will not join a board but will do one small thing. The board could ask for a welcomer who sends the welcome letter when a home changes hands, a meeting host who starts the video call, and a website keeper who looks at the site once a month.

A handover page for every job

When a role changes hands, the new person reads this page and the board guide and is ready. Nothing lives only in one person’s head.

Pay only for what is left

If the board does hire help, this page shows what the help would actually be doing: bookkeeping, invoices and contractors. The routine communication is already handled.

Letters already written

Copy one, fill in the parts in [brackets], and send. Please check each against the Bylaws once before the first use.

Annual meeting notice

Send at least 15 days before the meeting.

Subject: Annual Meeting of Members, [day, date] at [time] Dear neighbors, The annual meeting of The Orange's Homeowners Association will be held on [day, date] at [time], [online on Google Meet / at address]. On the agenda: election of directors, next year's budget, the financial statement, and your questions. Every household has one vote. If you cannot attend, you may vote by proxy: reply to this email and we will send the form. The link to join and the documents are on the website: [web address] The Board

Dues reminder

A friendly note before the due date.

Subject: A reminder about this year's dues Dear neighbor, This is a friendly reminder that the annual assessment of $[amount] is due on [date]. Please make the check out to The Orange's Homeowners Association, Inc. and mail it to P.O. Box 613, Silver Spring, MD 20918-0613. Write your street address on the memo line. If paying on time will be hard this year, write back. Talking to us early is always better than a late fee. Thank you, [name], Treasurer

Welcome to the neighborhood

For a household that has just moved in.

Subject: Welcome to The Orange's Dear [name], Welcome to the neighborhood. We are 33 homes, and the association is run by neighbors who volunteer. Everything you need is on our website: [web address]. It has trash and recycling days, how dues work, the rules in plain words, and a form to use before you change anything on the outside of your home. If you have a question, write to orangeshoa@gmail.com and a neighbor will answer. Glad you are here, The Board

We received your request

You can expect an answer by [date]. Please wait for a written decision before the work starts. If we need a photo or a measurement, we will write to you. You can see where your request stands on the website: [web address] The Architectural Control Committee
