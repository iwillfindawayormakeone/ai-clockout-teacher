How to enjoy these things well.

Care and safety for everything we make, and a plain-language glossary so words like natural, stabilized, and lab-created mean something. This is the same information printed on our care cards.

The first burn decides everything.

Wax has memory. Blow a candle out before the melted pool reaches the edge of the jar and it will tunnel down the middle for the rest of its life. Give the first burn two to four hours.

  • Trim the wick to a quarter inch before every burn. A long wick makes a tall flame, black smoke, and a hot jar.
  • Burn 2 to 4 hours at a time. Long enough for a full melt pool, not so long that the glass overheats.
  • Stop at half an inch of wax. Below that, the flame heats the glass base directly.
  • Keep it on a heat-safe surface, away from drafts, curtains, and anything that can catch.
  • Rough top after the first burn? Normal with natural waxes. It doesn't affect the next burn.

The three warnings on every label

These follow the candle fire-safety labeling standard, ASTM F2058, which the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission and the National Candle Association point makers to.

  • Burn within sight. Never leave a burning candle unattended.
  • Keep away from things that catch fire.
  • Keep away from children and pets.

If a candle ever worries you (a flame taller than an inch and a half, heavy soot, a jar too hot to touch), put it out and ask a question. We would rather replace it than have you burn it.

What “natural” actually means, and what we tell you.

The Federal Trade Commission's Jewelry Guides require sellers to disclose gemstone treatments that aren't permanent, need special care, or affect value. We state the treatment status of every stone, every time.

Natural, untreated
Mined, cut, and polished. Nothing else done to it. Most of our quartz and tourmaline.
Heated
Warmed to improve or change color. Common and stable in amethyst and citrine; most citrine on the market is heated amethyst. We say so if ours is.
Dyed
Color added. Common in howlite sold as turquoise, in agate, and in cheap jade. Dye can fade or rub off. We never sell dyed stones as anything but dyed.
Stabilized
Soft stone, often turquoise, set with resin so it can be cut and worn. Legitimate when disclosed.
Coated
A surface film, like the rainbow on aura quartz. Beautiful, not natural, and it can scratch off.
Lab-created
Same chemistry as the mined stone, grown in a lab. Real material, not mined. Always labeled.
Imitation
Glass, resin, or a different stone made to look like another. We don't sell imitations as gemstones.
Cultured (pearls)
Grown in a farmed mollusk. Nearly all pearls sold today are cultured. White freshwater pearls are routinely bleached and polished; we say so.

How we talk about meaning. Stones have carried meaning in many traditions for a long time, and that's a lovely reason to wear one. We won't promise a bracelet will change your health, money, relationships, or mind; health claims need evidence under FTC rules, and nobody has it for a bracelet. So you'll see “traditionally associated with,” “a reminder of,” and “for a personal ritual.” An invitation, not a guarantee.

About certificates of authenticity. A printed certificate proves nothing for an everyday stone like rose quartz, and collectors know it. What helps: a clear photo of the real beads, a named source, an honest treatment disclosure, and a maker who answers questions.

Make a bracelet last for years, not months.

Roll it on like a sock

Roll a stretch bracelet over your hand. Pulling it open with two fingers is how elastic dies. If it's too tight to roll, it's the wrong size, and we'll swap it.

Water, sweat, and perfume

Take jewelry off for swimming, showering, workouts, and sleeping. Put it on after lotion and perfume. Chlorine dulls stones and weakens cord; perfume clouds pearls permanently.

Cleaning and storage

Wipe with a soft dry cloth. Sterling tarnishes naturally; a polishing cloth brings it back in seconds. Store pieces separately in their pouch so stones don't scratch each other.

Cord snapped? Bead loose? Every bracelet is double-strung and pull-tested, but elastic is a wear item. We restring any bracelet we made for free in the first year, and for the cost of postage after that. Repair policy.

A bar that dries between uses lasts twice as long.

  • Use a draining dish. A bar sitting in water turns to mush by Tuesday.
  • Cut a large bar in half and keep the other half wrapped and dry.
  • Essential-oil scents soften with time. Use within a year for full scent; the soap stays good much longer.
  • Every ingredient is on the wrapper, listed by weight, including the sodium hydroxide used to make it. None remains in a cured bar.
  • Patch-test if you have sensitive skin, and stop if anything irritates.

Material Monday.

One material, explained plainly, every week. Posted in the Circle first and collected here.

Wax

Why coconut-soy, not paraffin or pure soy?

Pure soy frosts and sets with rough tops; paraffin throws scent hard but soots more easily in a small room. A coconut-soy blend burns cleanly, holds fragrance, and stays creamy in the jar. It also costs more, which is part of why the candle does.

Stone

Rose quartz is pink because of titanium.

The soft pink comes from microscopic titanium-bearing fibers, which is why it's rarely transparent and never uniform. Very pale beads are still rose quartz. Hot-pink, perfectly even beads usually aren't.

Metal

Gold-filled is not gold-plated.

Gold-filled has a thick layer of real gold bonded to brass, about a hundred times thicker than plating, and lasts years of daily wear. Plating can fade in months. We use gold-filled for clasps and say so.
